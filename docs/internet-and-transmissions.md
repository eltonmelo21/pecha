# The Internet, Texts and Transmissions in Tibetan Buddhism

**English** · [Português](internet-e-transmissoes.md)

There is no consensus among Tibetan teachers about using the internet to share texts, teachings, oral transmissions, empowerments and pūjās. Senior masters of the same school, and sometimes of the same lineage, take different paths.

This caution comes from the Vajrayāna, the tantric form of Buddhism, which also exists in other traditions, such as Shingon in Japan. A characteristic of the Tibetan Vajrayāna is to require three things before one begins many practices: the empowerment (དབང་, wang), the oral transmission (ལུང་, lung) and the instruction on the practice (ཁྲིད་, tri). This also applies to many pūjās, which assume that participants have already received these three steps.

Before online activities, pioneer publishers focused on Tibetan Buddhism, such as Snow Lion, Wisdom, Shambhala, Rangjung Yeshe and Padma Publishing, already made clear whom each book was meant for. Introductory and Mahāyāna books came out for a wide audience, and Vajrayāna books carried a restriction notice, as in [*Yeshe Lama*](https://www.shambhala.com/yeshe-lama.html), [*Treasury of Esoteric Instructions*](https://www.shambhala.com/treasury-of-esoteric-instructions.html) and [*The Roar of Thunder*](https://wisdomexperience.org/product/the-roar-of-thunder/).

The COVID-19 pandemic moved much of in-person activity online around the world, and spiritual practice was no different. With travel and in-person gatherings suspended, many teachers began using Zoom and YouTube to stay in touch with their students and to ensure that the transmissions would continue into the future.

Since then, it has become more common for saṅghas to keep groups on WhatsApp or Telegram. Zoom links and practice materials circulate there, in a more reserved way than an open post on a website. People who live far from practice centers are among those who have benefited most from this change.

Today the picture is varied. Some teachers offer only open teachings online. Others also give oral transmissions, and some give empowerments. Many centers stream pūjās live, and these pūjās end up being watched by anyone with internet access, even without the refuge vow, the bodhisattva vow, the empowerment, the oral transmission or the instruction on that practice.

**In this project**
The pecha.skill library uses [Lotsawa House](https://www.lotsawahouse.org) as its source and includes only public texts, without the restricted mark, published openly under the Creative Commons [CC BY-NC 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/) licence. Those who have the appropriate permissions for a practice text can use the skill with it, taking due care beforehand.

## Written Texts and How the Collections Mark Restrictions

The major collections of Tibetan Buddhist translations publish much of their material openly on the internet. Each has its own way of indicating when a text is restricted, that is, intended for those who have already received the corresponding empowerment, oral transmission and instruction.

**[Lotsawa House](https://www.lotsawahouse.org)**
A restricted text carries a red "Restricted" tag, on its own page and in the site's lists. Tapping it opens a notice explaining that the Vajrayāna limits the reading and practice of certain texts and suggesting that readers consult a lineage holder when in doubt. The text remains available to read. The [site's policy](https://www.lotsawahouse.org/about/restricted-texts-policy) says that decisions follow the advice of lineage holders, especially Alak Zenkar Rinpoche.

**[84000](https://84000.co)**
In the catalog, a restricted text appears with a warning triangle. When one of these texts is opened for the first time, a "Tantra Text Warning" notice appears, recalling the restrictions and commitments of tantra. The [site's policy](https://84000.co/documents/restricted-texts) explains that many senior teachers were consulted and that most considered open publication the best solution, leaving the responsibility with the reader.

**[Khyentse Vision Project](https://www.khyentsevision.org)**
A Vajrayāna text opens with a "Vajrayāna Text" notice, which closes only after the "I understand and accept" button. Some texts go further and require an account on the site: the reader confirms having received the prerequisites and requests access, which the project reviews before granting it. According to the site, the prerequisites are sometimes written in the text itself, at the beginning or in the colophon.

**[Samye Translations](https://www.samyetranslations.org)**
Every translation carries the same "Important Information" notice next to the PDFs, saying that the material may contain restricted practices. The site does not mark texts one by one, and the judgment rests with the reader and their teacher.

**[FPMT](https://fpmt.org)**
In the store, the description of a restricted text begins with "Restricted" and names the required empowerment; when ordering, the buyer confirms having met the prerequisites. On the practice pages, materials are separated into unrestricted ones and those restricted to people who have received the empowerment.

**Other traditions**
This page focuses on Tibetan Buddhism. Other traditions also have initiatives to digitize and translate their texts, such as [SuttaCentral](https://suttacentral.net) for the Theravāda, [CBETA](https://cbeta.org/en) for the Chinese canon and the [SAT Daizōkyō](https://21dzk.l.u-tokyo.ac.jp/SAT/index_en.html) for the canon used in Japan. In English, [BDK America](https://www.bdkamerica.org/tripitaka-list/), formerly the Numata Center, translates the Chinese canon in the BDK English Tripiṭaka series, and [Kalavinka Press](https://www.kalavinkapress.org) publishes translations of classics from the Indian and Chinese traditions, several of them as free e-books.

## Vajrayāna Teachers and Online Activities

This is only a general overview. Many other groups, saṅghas and teachers also hold online activities, and the names below are just a few examples of teachers who take different approaches to using the internet for Vajrayāna practice. The types of activity vary: teachings, oral transmissions, empowerments and pūjās. Not all of them give empowerments online, and the conditions also differ from one to another. Some accept only live transmission and do not record; others allow reception through a recording.

**Nyingma**
- The Vajrayana Foundation and Tulku Thadral Rinpoche have done important work in the [transmission of the Dudjom Tersar treasures](https://dudjomtersarngondro.com/archives/tag/tulku-thadral-rinpoche), with teachings and oral transmissions also streamed live on YouTube. They also offer the [transmission of the Rinchen Terdzö](https://www.namobudha.org/events/rinchen-terdzo-empowerments-and-transmissions/), with the option to follow it live.
- Shechen Rabjam Rinpoche gives online [oral transmissions of major collections](https://shechen.org/wp/2026/03/rabjam-rinpoche-lungs-to-be-live-streamed-april-1-7/), live only and without recording. Empowerments remain in person.
- Dzongsar Khyentse Rinpoche gives [live teachings](https://siddharthasintent.org/events/yayom-buddha-pada/), with interpretation into several languages, including Portuguese. His community maintains an [online temple](https://siddharthasintent.org/online-temple/) with practices and pūjās open to all, some only for those who have the empowerment.
- He also leads live pūjās in a way somewhat different from the traditional Tibetan form, such as the [Triratna](https://siddharthasintent.org/liturgies/triratna-sadhana/), [Tārā](https://siddharthasintent.org/events/21-day-tara-puja-and-teachings/) and [Uṣṇīṣavijayā](https://siddharthasintent.org/events/ushnisha-vijaya-vancouver/) pūjās, the last recited in English and Chinese. The choice is deliberate: he often warns of the risk of the Dharma becoming [bound to the elements of Asian culture](https://www.buddhistdoor.net/features/demographics-destiny-and-dharma-an-interview-with-dzongsar-khyentse-rinpoche/) and often recalls Chögyam Trungpa Rinpoche as a [pioneer in bringing the Dharma to Westerners](https://www.chronicleproject.com/dzongsar-jamyang-khyentse-rinpoche-celebrates-chogyam-trungpa-and-the-kagyu-lineage/).
- Jigme Khyentse Rinpoche has given teachings and oral transmissions online, with emphasis on Great Perfection texts connected to Khangsar Tenpé Wangchuk, such as [*The Natural Openness and Freedom of the Mind*](https://www.shambhala.com/jigme-khyentse-rinpoche-transmission/). He has also transmitted online a [guru yoga by Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo](https://www.khyentsevision.org/news/the-power-of-guru-yoga-khyentse-wangpo-anniversary-event/).

**Kagyü**
- In the Karma Kagyü tradition, the Karmapa Ogyen Trinley Dorje streams teachings and [empowerments live](https://www.youtube.com/live/u4dfRN8cZZc) on his official YouTube channel. Tai Situ Rinpoche gives teachings online, including on [Mahāmudrā](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ediqbOAxAC0).
- Mingyur Rinpoche, of the Karma Kagyü and Nyingma traditions, maintains the [Vajrayana Online](https://vajrayana.tergar.org/transmissions) platform, probably the most complete one available today, from the basics to the most advanced levels. He does not give empowerments online: the advanced levels are for those who have received the appropriate empowerments and transmissions in person, and the platform's [oral transmissions](https://vajrayana.tergar.org/reading-transmissions) are live only.
- Tergar has also held a hybrid retreat, the [Path of Liberation, Levels 1 and 2](https://www.even3.com.br/retiro-caminho-da-liberacao-niveis-1-2-no-brasil-741355/). Mingyur Rinpoche taught live from the United States, and Khenpo Kunga, present in Brazil, gave the empowerments and oral transmissions to the practitioners gathered there.
- Drikung Kyabgön Chetsang Rinpoche, Garchen Rinpoche, Khenpo Samdup Rinpoche and Drupon Rinchen Dorjee Rinpoche, of the Drikung Kagyü tradition, offer online [oral transmissions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cMH3vzlP5LI), [empowerments](https://pemakhandro.org/upcoming-programs/1415/vajrayogini-retreat-online-with-drupon-rinchen-dorjee-rinpoche-and-pema-khandro/) and teachings, often [free of charge](https://gardrolma.org/the-precious-shitro-empowerment-and-phowa-transmission/). Garchen Rinpoche even asks participants to [receive by video the empowerment](https://garchen.net/annual-events/) required before a group practice.
- Ga Lhakhang Rinpoche, of the Drukpa Kagyü tradition, gives [empowerments on Zoom](https://galhakhang.org/announcement-of-the-kurukulle-empowerment/), also streamed on YouTube. The sessions have simultaneous interpretation into other languages, such as Russian and Italian.
- The Karmapa Ogyen Trinley Dorje guides the editorial work of [Dharma Ebooks](https://dharmaebooks.org), an open library of Buddhist e-books in many languages.
- The late Thrangu Rinpoche founded [Namo Buddha Publications](https://namobuddhapublications.org), with several free e-books. Its [YouTube channel](https://youtube.com/@namobuddhapublications) holds an important archive of his teachings, including advanced ones.

**Sakya**
- The 41st Sakya Gongma Trichen and the 43rd Sakya Trizin give [empowerments live over the internet](https://sakyatemple.org/hhst-online-programs-2022/), with interpretation into several languages. Some are streamed by [Sakya Friends](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LyBCVzHgH6M), at the invitation of Lama Choedak Rinpoche.
- Lama Kunga Thartse Rinpoche gives [empowerments on Zoom only](https://www.ewamchoden.org/?p=26901), with clear rules: latecomers are not admitted, and those who leave before the end are not considered to have received the empowerment.
- Khenpo Migmar Tseten offers empowerments, transmissions and [teachings online](https://mangalamstudio.com/news/). He also keeps an online course with transmission and teachings on video and podcast.
- [Sakya Tradition](https://sakyatradition.org) keeps an online library of teachings and prayers, mainly from the Sakya throne holders.

**Gelug**
- The Dalai Lama has given [empowerments over the internet](https://www.dalailama.com/news/2020/preliminaries-for-an-avalokiteshvara-empowerment). On that occasion, he said he was confident that those with a clear intention to receive the empowerment do receive it, and that what matters most is a pure motivation.
- These online empowerments were publicly criticized. In 2020, a well-known lama questioned their validity and, a few days later, [withdrew the criticism](https://www.tibetanreview.net/lama-reportedly-apologized-to-dalai-lama-after-assailing-the-latter-for-giving-empowerments-online/), saying he was following the guidance of his masters, the Sakya Trichen and the then Sakya Trizin, Ratna Vajra Rinpoche.

**Jonang**
- Khentrul Rinpoche, a Jonang master of Kālacakra, offers online empowerments, oral transmissions, teachings and group practices. The [Kālacakra empowerment](https://dzokden.org/events/online-kalachakra-empowerment/) is live only, without recording.
